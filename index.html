01Research

Dissertation

Framing Psychosis: News Media, Stigma, and the Mechanisms of Public Response in Ghana

The dissertation asks how news coverage of psychosis shapes what readers believe about the illness, how they feel toward people living with it, and whether they would recommend professional help. It pairs a content analysis of a decade of Ghanaian coverage with a framing experiment.

Committee: Nikki McClaran (Chair), Leslie Steeves, S. S. Ofori-Parku, Deepu Murty

Study 1 · Content analysis

How Ghanaian news covers psychosis

A quantitative content analysis of 332 stories from 10 outlets, 2015–2025, coded for frames, attributions of cause and responsibility, and stigmatizing language. The corpus was built from Factiva, Nexis Uni, and web scraping, and screened for relevance with an LLM-assisted pipeline.

Study 2 · Framing experiment

What those frames do to readers

A three-condition experiment (fear, empathy, neutral frames) comparing two routes from a news story to stigma: how controllable readers judge the illness to be, and how they feel toward the person living with it. Stigma, in turn, shapes whether readers would recommend help.

New Jersey public schools show pandemic recovery

Across 668 districts and six measures of school performance, most districts held steady or improved between 2018–19 and 2020–21. The gains were not shared evenly.

By Stephen M. Anti · Data: New Jersey Department of Education School Performance Reports

Attendance and SAT scores rose. College enrollment fell.

The six measures moved in different directions. Nearly every district reduced the number of students missing six to ten days of school, and two in three raised SAT scores and kept their teaching staff more experienced. Postsecondary enrollment was the exception: four in five districts sent fewer graduates on to college or training.

Graduation rates split down the middle

For four-year graduation rates, 151 districts improved and 151 declined. The largest swings were more than ten percentage points in each direction.

Absenteeism fell almost everywhere

97% of districts reduced the number of students absent for six to ten days. Greenwich Township went from an average of 48 such students to 6. Sea Girt Borough had the largest increase, from 32 to 45.

How the analysis was done

I used the New Jersey Department of Education's annual School Performance Reports and adjusted cohort graduation rates, selecting the six indicators that had values for both 2018–19 and 2020–21: graduation rate, SAT scores, teacher experience, dropout rate, postsecondary enrollment, and days absent. After removing districts with missing values, 668 remained. For each district I calculated the change on each measure and classified the district by whether it improved or declined on a majority of the six.

The reporting includes interviews with Megan Kuhfeld (NWEA), Julia Sass Rubin and Saul Rubinstein (Rutgers University), Steve Baker (New Jersey Education Association), and students and parents in Monroe Township.
